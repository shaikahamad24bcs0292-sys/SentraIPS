NIST Special Publication 800-77 Revision 1
Guide to IPsec VPNs
National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

[Section 3.1: Cryptographic Algorithms for IPsec]
NIST recommends the use of Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data (AEAD)
ciphers for all IPsec communications. Advanced Encryption Standard in Galois/Counter
Mode (AES-GCM) with 128-bit or 256-bit keys (AES-128-GCM and AES-256-GCM) is strongly
recommended for both IKEv2 and ESP payloads.

Legacy symmetric block ciphers operating in Cipher Block Chaining (CBC) mode, such as
AES-CBC or 3DES-CBC, require separate integrity algorithms (e.g., HMAC-SHA-256) and are
susceptible to padding oracle vulnerabilities if improperly validated. Non-AEAD modes
SHOULD be transitioned to AEAD ciphers wherever possible. Unauthenticated encryption
modes or ciphers with key lengths under 128 bits MUST NOT be deployed.

[Section 3.2: Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange Groups]
Key establishment security depends on the mathematical strength of the Diffie-Hellman
(DH) group selected during IKE negotiation. Agencies MUST use Diffie-Hellman groups
that provide at least 112 bits of equivalent security strength, with 128 bits or higher
strongly recommended for sensitive communications.

Approved Diffie-Hellman groups:
- Group 14: 2048-bit Modular Exponential (MODP) Group (minimum acceptable for legacy MODP)
- Group 15: 3072-bit MODP Group
- Group 16: 4096-bit MODP Group
- Group 19: 256-bit Random Elliptic Curve (ecp256 / NIST P-256) (Recommended)
- Group 20: 384-bit Random Elliptic Curve (ecp384 / NIST P-384) (Recommended)

Deprecated and Insecure Diffie-Hellman groups:
- Group 1 (768-bit MODP): INSECURE. MUST NOT be used.
- Group 2 (1024-bit MODP): INSECURE. Broken under modern cryptanalysis. MUST NOT be used.
- Group 5 (1536-bit MODP): LEGACY / INSUFFICIENT. Provides under 100 bits of security strength.
Agencies MUST transition away from DH Group 5 to Group 14 or Elliptic Curve Group 19.

[Section 3.3: Perfect Forward Secrecy (PFS)]
Perfect Forward Secrecy (PFS) ensures that the compromise of long-term gateway private
keys or IKE SA master keys does not compromise previously recorded session traffic.
When PFS is enabled, an independent ephemeral Diffie-Hellman key exchange is performed
during the creation and rekeying of each Child Security Association (Child SA).

NIST SP 800-77 Rev. 1 mandates that IPsec gateways MUST enable Perfect Forward Secrecy
(PFS) for all Child SAs in high-security and enterprise VPN deployments. Deployments
operating with PFS disabled are vulnerable to retroactive decryption if the long-term
private key or IKE SA keys are later obtained by an adversary.

[Section 3.4: IKE Protocol Versions and Authentication]
Agencies MUST migrate all IPsec VPNs to Internet Key Exchange Protocol Version 2 (IKEv2,
RFC 7296). IKEv1 is deprecated due to structural weaknesses, high handshake latency,
and susceptibility to offline dictionary attacks during aggressive mode negotiation.
IKEv2 provides improved resilience against denial-of-service (DoS) attacks, native NAT
traversal support, simplified rekeying, and robust certificate authentication.

[Section 3.5: Traffic Flow Confidentiality (TFC)]
To mitigate side-channel traffic analysis where eavesdroppers infer application behavior
or data volume from packet sizes and timings, IPsec gateways SHOULD implement Traffic
Flow Confidentiality (TFC) padding as specified in RFC 4303. Adding randomized padding
masks packet size distributions and resists passive machine learning classification.
